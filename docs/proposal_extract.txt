SikhyaSetu

Format: software-only, local-first PWA + cloud agent backend
Primary learner: Sunita, Class 9, Odia-medium, shared phone, unreliable connectivity
One-line pitch: SikhyaSetu turns every wrong answer into a diagnosis, every diagnosis into a tiny offline lesson, and 
every offline session into the teacher’s next best action — in Odia and within the learner’s data budget.
1. Executive verdict
Is the current idea strong?
Yes — the core idea is genuinely differentiated and technically credible. The strongest parts of the attached concept are:
1. Compile online, serve offline: cloud agents prepare a compact personalised lesson pack; the phone runs it without a live model 
or signal. 
2. Misconception diagnosis rather than scorekeeping: the product asks why a learner is wrong and selects a counter-example 
or prerequisite lesson. 
3. Deferred intelligence: voice teach-back can be recorded offline and graded when connectivity returns. 
4. Data- and time-aware planning: the next pack is sized around the learner’s available megabytes and study window. 
5. A real agent loop: events → diagnosis → mastery update → plan → pack → next session. 
What would weaken it in a competitive field?
The original document lists seven strong ideas, but gives them similar prominence. A judge may remember none of them, or may conclude 
that the team is promising too much:
• peer-to-peer sharing, peer voice notes, try-it verification, live doubt solving, nudges, shared-phone profiles, voice, teacher 
analytics, and a full cloud agent loop are too much for one hackathon MVP; 
• the phrase “AI tutor” is common, so the differentiator must be the offline misconception loop, not the existence of a chatbot; 
• several integrations are described before the minimum end-to-end proof is locked; 
• the product needs explicit acceptance criteria and a fallback demo path. 
Recommended strategic decision
Keep the architecture, narrow the story. Make one loop the hero:
Probe → explain → teach back → diagnose misconception → compile the next offline pack.
Make three distinctive MVPs visible:
1. Misconception Fingerprint: a structured learner state that records the likely wrong mental model, not merely a score. 
2. Learning-per-MB Compiler: an agent that chooses the most valuable next content under a data and time budget. 
3. Teacher Action Brief: a class-level summary that turns aggregated misconceptions into one suggested five-minute classroom 
intervention. 
Shared-phone profiles remain an important enabling feature. Peer sharing, try-it tasks, WhatsApp nudges, and live free-form doubt 
answering become roadmap items unless already working.
2. The problem
Sunita is a Class 9 learner in an Odia-medium setting. She may have access to a shared Android phone for roughly one hour in the 
evening, but not to a stable internet connection, unlimited data, or an always-available tutor.
Existing learning apps often assume:
• continuous connectivity; 
• video-heavy consumption; 
• individual device ownership; 
• comfortable English reading and typing; 
• a teacher who can interpret a score and decide what to do next.

3. Product thesis: one loop, three innovations
3.1 The hero loop: Misconception-to-Mastery
1. Probe: the learner answers a carefully designed question. 
2. Fingerprint: the selected distractor or teach-back is mapped to a likely misconception. 
3. Intervene: the offline tutor serves a counter-example, analogy, prerequisite, or worked example. 
4. Teach back: the learner explains the idea in her own words; audio can be queued offline. 
5. Sync and grade: when a connection returns, the cloud grader evaluates the teach-back against a rubric. 
6. Re-plan: the planner chooses the next concept and compiles a new compact pack. 
This is not “chat with an LLM.” The agents create a closed learning system that changes what the learner sees next.
3.2 MVP 1 — Misconception Fingerprint
Each learner has a small, explainable state per concept:
• current mastery estimate; 
• likely misconception label; 
• evidence: probe choice, confidence, time, teach-back rubric; 
• recommended intervention; 
• confidence in the diagnosis; 
• next review date. 
Example:
Concept: force and motion
Observed error: treats force as something an object “contains”
Evidence: selected distractor B + teach-back phrase pattern
Intervention: bicycle-braking counter-example + one prerequisite micro-lesson
Next check: same concept with a new context after spaced revision
This gives judges a concrete answer to “How is this more than a quiz app?”
3.3 MVP 2 — Learning-per-MB Compiler
The Planner/Compiler agent does not simply generate a long lesson. It selects the highest-value learning items under constraints:
• estimated available data; 
• learner’s typical session duration; 
• unresolved misconceptions; 
• prerequisites; 
• spaced-revision needs; 
• audio/text size; 
• content confidence and review status.

===== PAGE 3 =====
A pack can contain text-first essentials, optional compressed audio, and a small number of high-information probes. The system can 
show a judge:
“We had 2.4 MB and 35 minutes. We prioritised the misconception repair and one prerequisite, deferred optional audio, 
and scheduled revision for tomorrow.”
This turns low bandwidth from a limitation into an explicit agent decision.
3.4 MVP 3 — Teacher Action Brief
The teacher view should not be a generic dashboard. It should answer one practical question:
What can the teacher do in the next five minutes to address the class’s biggest misconception?
The brief contains:
• top three misconception clusters; 
• number of learners affected, shown as a count or bucket rather than personally identifying data; 
• one suggested classroom prompt or demonstration; 
• learners needing follow-up, if consent and identification are enabled; 
• what the planner will send in the next pack cycle. 
Example:
“12 learners are treating current as something that gets used up. Use the two-bulb comparison prompt, then ask learners 
to explain where the brightness changes. SikhyaSetu will send a counter-example pack to the affected profiles.”

5. User experience
Learner flow — offline
1. Select profile: large icon/avatar; no photo required. 
2. Today’s plan: “2 concepts, 25 minutes, 0.8 MB already downloaded.” 
3. Learn: short Odia explanation with a local example; English technical term shown where useful. 
4. Probe: one question with distractors linked to misconceptions. 
5. Diagnosis: “Your answer suggests this idea is mixed up…” in respectful language. 
6. Repair: counter-example, analogy, or prerequisite mini-lesson. 
7. Teach back: record a short explanation in Odia; show “saved for next sync.” 
8. Progress: show concept status locally, without pretending that deferred voice grading is complete. 
Online reconnect flow
1. Sync events using unique IDs and retry-safe batches. 
2. Transcribe and grade queued teach-backs. 
3. Update the Misconception Fingerprint. 
4. Generate the Teacher Action Brief. 
5. Run the planner under a data/time budget. 
6. Compile and deliver the next signed pack. 
Language design
• Native Odia generation or human-reviewed demo content; do not present raw machine translation as finished UX. 
• Short sentences, large type, audio-first controls, minimal typing. 
• Local examples such as paddy fields, hand pumps, bicycles, rainfall, and household electricity. 
• Code-mixing only where it helps connect classroom terminology to Odia explanation. 
• Respectful diagnosis: “Let’s check this idea again,” not “You are wrong.” 
6. Architecture and agency
SikhyaSetu has two layers with a clear division of responsibility:
Layer 1 — The phone: reliable offline execution
The phone contains the learner’s profiles, downloaded lesson packs, local progress, and queued events. A small deterministic tutor runs 
the pack without an internet connection:
Select profile → teach → probe → diagnose → intervene → record → log
The phone does not need to call an LLM during the lesson. This makes the core experience fast, predictable, and usable in airplane mode.
Layer 2 — The cloud: adaptive planning when connectivity returns
The cloud receives the phone’s events and performs the work that needs deeper reasoning:
Sync → grade teach-back → update learner state → plan → optimise budget → compile pack → deliver
The cloud also generates the class-level Teacher Action Brief from aggregated misconception evidence.
End-to-end data flow
Step What happens Where
1. Prepare The planner chooses concepts, prerequisites, and revision items Cloud
2. Compile The compiler creates a small, structured Odia lesson pack within the MB/time budget Cloud
3. Download The signed pack is downloaded in a small, resumable transfer Phone ↔ cloud
4. Learn offline The deterministic tutor delivers the lesson, probe, diagnosis, and intervention Phone
5. Collect evidence Answers, time, progress, and teach-back audio are stored locally Phone
6. Reconnect Events and queued audio are synchronised safely using unique IDs Phone ↔ cloud

Why not put an LLM on the phone?
A live on-device model would make the demo fragile, increase storage and performance requirements, and make Odia quality difficult to 
guarantee. SikhyaSetu instead compiles intelligence when a connection exists and executes a tested pack offline.
7. Pack and event design
Section 7 defines the two small data objects that make the local-first design possible:
1. The lesson pack: what the phone needs in order to teach offline. 
2. The event log: what the phone records so the cloud can adapt the next session. 
7.1 What is inside a lesson pack?
A lesson pack is a compact, signed, learner-specific bundle. It is not a full textbook and it does not contain a live chatbot. It contains only 
the next best learning experience selected by the planner.
Pack component Purpose
Pack ID and version Identifies the learner’s current pack and prevents old content from overwriting new content
Language and reading 
level Keeps explanations appropriate for the learner and the selected language
Learning objectives Defines the concepts the learner should work on in this session
Short explanations Provides text and, where available, reviewed Odia audio
Local examples Connects the concept to familiar situations such as bicycles, rainfall, farming, or household electricity

Pack component Purpose
Probe questions Tests understanding with answer choices linked to specific misconceptions
Intervention cards Contains the counter-example, analogy, worked example, or prerequisite lesson to show after an error
Teach-back prompt Asks the learner to explain the concept in her own words
Budget metadata Records the pack’s expected size in KB and expected completion time

7.2 How the pack is used offline
Before connectivity is lost, the cloud delivers the pack to the phone. During the lesson, the phone follows the pack in a predictable order:
Load pack → show explanation → ask probe → map response to misconception → show intervention → request teach-back → 
save progress
The phone can complete this entire flow without calling an LLM. This is why the demo continues to work in airplane mode.
7.3 What is an event?
An event is a small record of something important that happened during the learner’s session. Events are written locally first and uploaded 
later.
Event Meaning
session_started The learner began a pack
probe_answered The learner selected an answer and, when available, confidence or time was recorded
intervention_viewe
d The learner received a targeted explanation or counter-example
teachback_recorded A voice or text explanation was saved for later grading
pack_completed The learner reached the end of the planned session
sync_succeeded A batch was accepted by the cloud
sync_failed A batch could not be uploaded and must be retried
7.4 How offline sync works
1. The phone writes each event immediately to IndexedDB. 
2. Each event receives a unique ID, so a retry cannot create a duplicate. 
3. When connectivity returns, the phone uploads events in small batches. 
4. The cloud acknowledges accepted events and processes the new evidence. 
5. The grader updates the Misconception Fingerprint. 
6. The planner compiles the next pack. 
7. The phone downloads the new pack and keeps the previous pack until the new one is verified. 
This means a dropped connection does not erase progress and does not force the learner to restart.

Live sequence
0:00–0:35 — Story and promise
Show Sunita’s constraint: one shared phone, weak connectivity, Odia-medium learning. State the one-line pitch.
0:35–1:45 — Offline learning
Select Sunita. Turn on airplane mode. Complete a lesson. Deliberately choose a wrong distractor. Show the misconception fingerprint and

1:45–2:20 — Teach-back
Record a short Odia explanation. Show that it is saved locally and queued, not falsely graded in the absence of a connection.
2:20–2:40 — Shared-phone mode
Switch to a second profile. Show that progress and packs are isolated.
2:40–4:15 — Agent reconnect
Restore connectivity. Show the event sync, deferred grading, mastery update, planner decision, budget decision, and newly compiled 
pack. Keep the trace human-readable:
“Because Sunita selected the force-as-substance distractor and has 35 minutes with 1.2 MB available, we prioritised

4:15–5:00 — Teacher Action Brief
Show the class misconception cluster and the suggested five-minute intervention. End by returning to the problem: the system does not 
only teach; it closes the loop between learner evidence, next content, and teacher action.
Demo backup path
If the live compiler or STT fails, switch to a seeded response while clearly labelling it as a fallback. The offline flow, trace, and pack 
transition must remain demonstrable without external provider availability.

12. Evaluation plan and measurable prototype targets
These are engineering targets, not claims of educational impact:
• Offline core flow starts and completes with network disabled. 
• At least 90% of seeded test events survive an interrupted sync without duplication or loss. 
• Every demo misconception has a deterministic mapping to an intervention. 
• New pack generation displays the selected concept, reason, estimated size, and time budget. 
• Teacher Action Brief is generated from actual demo events, not a static screenshot. 
• All demo Odia text/audio is reviewed by a native speaker or clearly labelled as prototype output.

A future pilot could measure misconception resolution across repeated probes, teach-back rubric improvement, completion rate under data 
limits, and teacher usefulness. Do not present those as proven during the hackathon.
13. Judge questions and strong answers
“Why is this agentic and not just a chatbot?”
Because the system observes events, updates a structured learner state, chooses the next concepts, optimises content under constraints, 
compiles an executable pack, and schedules follow-up. The offline tutor is the reliable executor; the cloud agents make the decisions that 
change the next session.
“Why not use an LLM offline?”
The product needs reliability on low-end phones and honest Odia quality. We compile the model’s work online into a tested pack, then run 
that pack offline. This is more practical and auditable than pretending a live generative tutor is always available.
“What is your most defensible innovation?”
The Misconception-to-Mastery loop under a data budget. Offline support alone is not new; diagnosis alone is not new. The 
combination closes the loop despite intermittent connectivity.
“What happens when the learner has no internet?”
She can complete the downloaded lesson, answer probes, receive deterministic interventions, record a teach-back, and continue. The 
intelligence that requires the cloud is deferred rather than silently fabricated.
“How do you handle incorrect AI output?”
Content is generated into a structured pack, constrained by a curated concept/misconception library, reviewed for the demo, and served 
with fallback templates. The phone does not freely generate high-stakes explanations offline.
“Can this scale beyond Odia?”
The pack schema, misconception state, and deterministic tutor are language-agnostic. The next language requires reviewed content, 
speech support, and local examples; it is a roadmap, not an unsupported current claim.
“How do you protect children’s data?”
Use pseudonymous learner IDs, collect minimal events, avoid photographs, separate aggregates from identifiable views, and make audio 
retention explicit. A real deployment would require institutional consent and a fuller child-data governance process.

15. Final positioning
The sentence to repeat throughout the pitch
SikhyaSetu is not an online chatbot that fails when the signal disappears. It is an agentic learning loop that uses 
the cloud to prepare the next best lesson and the phone to deliver it offline.
The three features judges should remember
1. Misconception Fingerprint: it identifies the wrong idea, not just the wrong answer. 
2. Learning-per-MB Compiler: it spends scarce data on the highest-value next step. 
3. Teacher Action Brief: it converts learner evidence into tomorrow’s five-minute classroom action. 
Final recommendation
Build the revised version. The original concept is strong enough to compete, but the winning version is smaller, sharper, more honest, 
and easier to prove live. Make the offline misconception loop unavoidable in the demo; treat everything else as support for that loop or as 
a clearly labelled roadmap.
